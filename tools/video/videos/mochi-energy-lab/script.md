# Mochi's Energy Lab: video script (draft 1)

**Topic:** Energy unit test study guide, TEKS 4.8A, 4.8B and 4.8C.
**Length:** about 3½–4 minutes.
**Cast:**
- **MOCHI**: the purple cat scientist from the Energy Lab page. Curious, upbeat, likes cat puns. Main narrator.
- **PIP**: a wind-up toy mouse who's eager but often gets things wrong. Mochi corrects him, so the video catches common mistakes: energy disappearing, wood conducting, a broken circuit still working.

*Lines in italics are what's on screen and are never read aloud. **On-screen text** shows as captions/labels.*

---

## Scene 1: Lights on! (≈20 s)

*A dark lab. A switch clicks, a bulb glows, and Mochi is standing there.*

**MOCHI:** Welcome to my Energy Lab! I'm Mochi, and today we're learning how energy moves, what it can travel through, and how circuits make things work.

*Pip zooms in, spinning, and crashes into a beaker.*

**PIP:** And I'm Pip! I'm a wind-up mouse, so I know everything about energy!

**MOCHI:** We'll see about that, Pip.

*On-screen title: **Energy is always on the move!***

---

## Scene 2: Energy transfer (≈60 s)

**MOCHI:** Big idea number one: energy can **transfer**. That means it moves from one object or place to another.

*Mochi bats a purple yarn ball. It rolls and bumps a yellow ball, and the yellow ball rolls away.*

**MOCHI:** Watch. When I bat this yarn ball, it moves. A moving object has **mechanical energy**, the energy of motion. When it bumps the yellow ball, the energy transfers, and now the yellow ball moves!

**PIP:** And then the energy disappears. Poof!

**MOCHI:** Nope! Energy never disappears. It just moves somewhere else, or changes into a different kind of energy.

*On-screen: **Energy doesn't disappear. It moves or changes!***

*Mochi drops a pebble into a pond. Ripples spread out.*

**MOCHI:** Energy moves through water, too. When a pebble splashes into a pond, its energy travels through the water as **waves**. Those ripples are energy on the move.

*Pip plucks a guitar string with his tail. The string shakes and sound waves wiggle toward Mochi's ear.*

**PIP:** Twang!

**MOCHI:** And that's **sound energy**. Sound happens when something **vibrates**, which means it shakes back and forth super fast. The vibrations travel through the air to our ears.

*On-screen: **Motion · Water waves · Sound***

---

## Scene 3: Conductors and insulators (≈55 s)

*Two signs appear: ⚡ CONDUCTOR and 🧤 INSULATOR.*

**MOCHI:** Big idea number two. Some materials let energy pass through easily. Those are **conductors**. Metals are great conductors, like copper, aluminum, and iron.

**MOCHI:** Other materials slow energy down or stop it. Those are **insulators**, like rubber, plastic, wood, and cloth.

*Pip holds up a wooden spoon.*

**PIP:** This spoon is a conductor, right?

**MOCHI:** Is it made of metal?

**PIP:** …It's made of tree.

**MOCHI:** Then it's an insulator!

*Quick sorting animation: copper wire, iron nail and foil fly to CONDUCTOR. Rubber boots, plastic straw and wool scarf fly to INSULATOR.*

**MOCHI:** Here's a trick: if it's metal, it's a conductor.

*A soup pot on a stove. Its metal bottom glows warm, and the plastic handle stays cool.*

**MOCHI:** Lots of things use both. A pot has a metal bottom to heat the soup fast, and a plastic handle so your paw doesn't get burned. And electric wires are metal inside, so electricity flows, with plastic outside to keep you safe.

---

## Scene 4: Circuits (≈55 s)

*A battery, wires, a switch, and a light bulb.*

**MOCHI:** Big idea number three: electricity travels in a path called a **circuit**. To build one, you need a **battery** for energy, **wires** for the path, and something to use the energy, like a bulb.

*Mochi flips the switch on. The bulb lights.*

**MOCHI:** When the path is complete, with no gaps, it's a **closed circuit**. Electricity flows, and the light turns on!

*Pip yanks a wire loose. The bulb goes dark.*

**PIP:** Oops.

**MOCHI:** Now there's a gap. That's an **open circuit**. Electricity can't flow, so the light goes off.

*On-screen: **Closed = no gaps, it works. Open = a gap, it stops.***

*Pip stuffs a wooden stick into the gap. Nothing happens.*

**PIP:** Fixed it!

**MOCHI:** Wood is an insulator, Pip. The circuit is still open. We need a conductor, like a metal paper clip.

*Mochi clips a paper clip into the gap. The bulb lights again.*

---

## Scene 5: Energy changes (≈35 s)

**MOCHI:** Last big idea: a circuit can change electrical energy into other kinds of energy.

*Each item appears and does its thing.*

**MOCHI:** A light bulb changes electrical energy into **light** and **thermal** energy. That's why bulbs feel warm.

**MOCHI:** A buzzer changes it into **sound** energy.

*BZZZT! Pip's fur stands up.*

**MOCHI:** And a fan changes it into **mechanical** energy, the energy of motion.

*The fan spins and Pip blows across the screen.*

**PIP:** Wheeeee!

---

## Scene 6: Quick quiz (≈40 s)

*On-screen: **Pop quiz!** Each question shows for a few seconds with a countdown before Mochi answers. These are the four practice questions from the teacher's study guide.*

**MOCHI:** Pop quiz! Say your answer out loud before I do.

**MOCHI:** One. What kind of energy do you get when a radio plays music?
*(3-second pause)*
**MOCHI:** Sound energy!

**MOCHI:** Two. Is metal a conductor or an insulator?
*(3-second pause)*
**MOCHI:** A conductor!

**MOCHI:** Three. What happens when there's a break in a circuit?
*(3-second pause)*
**MOCHI:** The circuit is open, so electricity can't flow.

**MOCHI:** Four. How do you know energy moved from one object to another?
*(3-second pause)*
**MOCHI:** You see a change! Something starts moving, makes a sound, gets warmer, or gives off light.

---

## Scene 7: Goodbye (≈15 s)

**MOCHI:** You're fully charged! Now try the quiz in my Energy Lab to earn some stars.

**PIP:** And remember: energy never disappears!

**MOCHI:** Pip… you were listening!

*Pip's wind-up key stops. He freezes mid-wave. Mochi taps him and he starts again. The end.*
