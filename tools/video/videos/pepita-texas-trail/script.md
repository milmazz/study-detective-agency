# Pepita's Texas Trail: video script (draft 1)

**Topic:** Grade 4 Social Studies, Unit 3: Exploration and Settlement of Texas.
**Length:** about 5–5½ minutes. The unit covers more ground than Energy did.
**Cast:**
- **PEPITA**: the explorer chick in the safari hat from the Texas Trail page. Upbeat, loves her map, makes egg puns. Main narrator.
- **DILLY**: a nine-banded armadillo, the Texas state small mammal. Eager and confident, but he mixes facts up the same way the teacher's wrong answer choices do. Pepita corrects him.

*Lines in italics are what's on screen and are never read aloud. **On-screen text** shows as captions/labels.*

---

## Scene 1: On the trail (≈25 s)

*A sunny trail across a map of Texas. Pepita walks in, unfolding a big map.*

**PEPITA:** Howdy, explorers! I'm Pepita, and today we're hiking the trail of early Texas history, from the first European explorers all the way to Austin's Colony.

*Dilly scurries in and tries to curl into a ball. He only gets halfway, then tips over.*

**DILLY:** And I'm Dilly! Watch me roll into a ball!

**PEPITA:** Dilly, Texas armadillos can't actually roll up.

**DILLY:** …I'm working on it.

*On-screen: four trail stops light up: 🧭 Explorers · ⛪ Missions · 🐄 New settlers · 📜 Timeline*

---

## Scene 2: European explorers (≈60 s)

*Stop 1 lights up. Three ships sail in with flags: two Spanish, one French.*

**PEPITA:** Stop one: European explorers. Why did countries like Spain and France send explorers? Three big reasons. **Wealth and trade**, because they wanted riches and new things to trade. **Competition**, because they were racing each other. And **expansion**, because they wanted more land and more power.

*Three cards pop up: 💰 Wealth & trade · 🏁 Competition · 🗺️ Expansion*

*Explorer cards flip over one at a time, with a dotted route drawn across the map for each.*

**PEPITA:** **Cabeza de Vaca** was shipwrecked on the Texas coast in 1528. He traveled through Texas for years and reported on the land, the animals, and the American Indian peoples he met.

**PEPITA:** **Francisco Coronado** crossed the plains of Texas in the 1540s. He met American Indian groups, and his journey gave Spain information about the region.

**PEPITA:** And **La Salle** led an expedition that built **Fort St. Louis** in Texas in 1685.

**DILLY:** Another Spanish explorer!

**PEPITA:** Nope, La Salle was **French**! And a French fort in Texas made Spain worried, so the competition between France and Spain grew.

---

## Scene 3: Sources and maps (≈45 s)

*Two piles appear: an old journal and a letter on one side, a textbook and a laptop on the other.*

**PEPITA:** How do we know all this? From **sources**. A **primary source** was made at the time, by someone who was there, like a journal, a letter, a map drawn on the trip, or an artifact.

**PEPITA:** A **secondary source** was made later to explain the past, like a textbook or a history website.

*Dilly holds up a dusty, cobweb-covered textbook.*

**DILLY:** My textbook is super old, so it's a primary source!

**PEPITA:** Old doesn't make it primary, Dilly. Was it written by someone who was there? No? Then it's secondary.

*A map appears. Each tool glows as Pepita names it.*

**PEPITA:** And when you read a map, remember: the **compass rose** shows directions, the **legend** explains the symbols, and the **scale** helps you figure out distance.

---

## Scene 4: Spanish missions (≈65 s)

*Stop 2 lights up. Mission buildings with bells appear along a river.*

**PEPITA:** Stop two: Spanish missions. A mission was a religious settlement. Spain built missions to **spread the Catholic faith** and to **support Spanish settlement** and power in Texas. Back then, Texas was part of a colony controlled by Spain. That's what **colonial** means.

*A pin drops near El Paso, then pins appear at San Antonio.*

**PEPITA:** **Mission Ysleta**, near El Paso, is recognized as the first mission in Texas. The San Antonio missions, including the **Alamo**, are important historic landmarks.

*A pin in the lower Rio Grande Valley.*

**PEPITA:** And **José de Escandón** helped set up settlements in the lower Rio Grande Valley.

*The river sparkles. Icons pop up along it: 🥤 💧 🌽 🐴 🛶*

**PEPITA:** Notice how settlements grew near rivers? People needed water for drinking, farming, animals, and travel.

*Two groups of icons drift toward each other in the middle: cattle, horses and wheat from one side, corn, beans and squash from the other.*

**PEPITA:** When Spanish settlers and American Indian peoples met, they influenced each other. They exchanged knowledge, crops, animals, and ways of doing things. That's **cultural interaction**, and it helped shape Texas culture. But not everyone experienced the missions the same way. Some people came for food and protection, and others did not want to change their way of life.

---

## Scene 5: Mexico and the empresarios (≈60 s)

*Stop 3 lights up. A calendar flips to 1821. The Mexican flag rises.*

**PEPITA:** Stop three! In **1821**, Mexico won its independence from Spain. Texas was now part of Mexico, and the new government made new rules about who could settle there.

**DILLY:** Oh, I know this one! Spain became independent from Mexico!

**PEPITA:** Other way around, Dilly! **Mexico** became independent from **Spain**.

*A scroll unrolls: EMPRESARIO.*

**PEPITA:** Mexico let **empresarios** bring settlers. An empresario was a person given permission, or a land grant, to bring settlers to Texas and start a colony.

*Covered wagons roll in, with families.*

**PEPITA:** The most famous empresario was **Stephen F. Austin**. He brought hundreds of Anglo-American families to Texas and set up **Austin's Colony**. Another empresario, **Martín De León**, founded the town of **Victoria**.

*Icons: 🏞️ land · 🌾 farm · 🤝 trade · 🏡 new life. Cows and pigs wander across the screen.*

**PEPITA:** Why did settlers come? To get land, farm, trade, and build a new life. Farming and raising livestock were important jobs for many early settlers.

---

## Scene 6: Timeline (≈40 s)

*Stop 4 lights up. Dilly is proudly holding four picture cards in the WRONG order: wagons first.*

**DILLY:** I made the timeline! Austin's Colony goes first!

**PEPITA:** Let's fix that. First came **European explorers**. Then **Spanish missions and settlements**. Then **Mexico won independence** in 1821. And then **empresarios** like Austin brought new settlers.

*The cards shuffle into the right order on a timeline, each with a little ✓.*

**PEPITA:** And each event helped cause the next. La Salle's French fort made Spain worry, so Spain built missions in East Texas. And Mexican independence led to new rules that let empresarios bring settlers.

*On-screen: **Cause → Effect** arrows between the cards.*

---

## Scene 7: Pop quiz (≈45 s)

*On-screen: **Pop quiz!** Each question shows for a few seconds with a countdown before Pepita answers. These are four of the practice questions from the teacher's study guide.*

**PEPITA:** Pop quiz! Say your answer out loud before I do.

**PEPITA:** One. Which explorer is connected to Fort St. Louis?
*(3-second pause)*
**PEPITA:** La Salle!

**PEPITA:** Two. What happened in 1821?
*(3-second pause)*
**PEPITA:** Mexico won independence from Spain!

**PEPITA:** Three. What did an empresario do?
*(3-second pause)*
**PEPITA:** Helped bring settlers to Texas.

**PEPITA:** Four. Which came earliest: Austin's Colony, Mexican independence, Spanish missions, or European exploration?
*(3-second pause)*
**PEPITA:** European exploration!

---

## Scene 8: Goodbye (≈15 s)

**PEPITA:** Egg-cellent work, explorer! Now try the quiz on my Texas Trail to earn some stars.

**DILLY:** And remember: explorers, missions, Mexico, then empresarios!

**PEPITA:** Dilly, you got it in order!

*Dilly is so proud he tries to curl into a ball again, gets halfway, and tips over. Pepita laughs. The end.*
