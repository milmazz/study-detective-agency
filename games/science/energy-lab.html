Energy on the move

Energy can transfer, which means it moves from one object or place to another. You can see it happen in motion, in water waves, and in sound. Try each one.

Energy doesn’t disappear. It moves to something else, or it changes into a different kind of energy.

Conductors and insulators

Some materials let energy pass through easily. Others slow it down or stop it.

⚡

Conductor

Lets thermal energy (heat) or electrical energy pass through easily. Most conductors are metals: copper, aluminum, iron, and steel.

🧤

Insulator

Does not let energy pass through easily. It protects us or slows energy down. Examples: rubber, plastic, wood, and cloth.

Where you see both at once

  • Electric wires: the metal inside is a conductor, so electricity flows. The plastic outside is an insulator, so it stays in the wire and keeps you safe.
  • A cooking pot: the metal pan is a conductor, so heat reaches the food fast. The plastic handle is an insulator, so your hand doesn’t get burned.

Circuits

Electricity travels in a path called a circuit. When the path is closed (no gaps), electricity flows. When the path is open (there’s a gap), it can’t flow. Build one below.

Parts of a simple circuit

  • Battery: the energy source
  • Wires: the path electricity travels
  • Bulb, buzzer, or motor: the receiver that uses the energy
  • No gaps: the path must be closed

Energy changes

  • Light bulb: electrical → light and thermal
  • Buzzer: electrical → sound
  • Fan: electrical → mechanical (motion)
