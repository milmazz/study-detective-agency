Mixed numbers and decimals

A mixed number has a whole number and a fraction, like 184100. The decimal point sits between the wholes and the parts. Pick one and see where each digit goes.

The denominator tells you how many places. Out of 10 → 1 digit after the point (tenths). Out of 100 → 2 digits after the point (hundredths). If the fraction is only a few hundredths, put a 0 in the tenths place: 184100 = 18.04, not 18.4.

Money is decimals

One dollar is one whole. Coins are parts of a dollar.

Dime = 0.10

10 dimes make $1, so a dime is 1 tenth of a dollar.

Penny = 0.01

100 pennies make $1, so a penny is 1 hundredth of a dollar.

Quarter = 0.25

A quarter is 25 cents, which is 25 hundredths of a dollar.

Place value: the ×10 pattern

Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right. Moving right, each place is one tenth of the place to its left. Tap any digit to find its value.

Expanded notation

Same digit, one place to the left = 10 times as much. In 4.44, the 4 in the ones place (4) is 10 times the 4 in the tenths place (0.4), which is 10 times the 4 in the hundredths place (0.04).
